# Customer interviews and demand validation

Use this when deciding whom to serve, which problem to solve, or how much to commit to the next experiment. Read the company mode, product, current event and relevant material first.

For a real business, request the actual customer segment, access channel or interview evidence needed for this decision. In a simulation, establish coherent fictional customers, tasks and alternatives and disclose those assumptions; the player need not know real prospective customers.

Ask about the most recent concrete occurrence: what triggered the task, how it is handled today, where the difficulty lies, its time or monetary cost, and who makes purchasing decisions. Past behavior is stronger evidence than liking an idea or expressing hypothetical willingness to pay. Contacting, recruiting or sending material follows the user's existing authorization.

Keep observations, quotations, interpretation and assumptions separate. Retain dates, relevant participant context and traceable material. Keep personal identifiers and access credentials out of reusable procedures. Explain sample selection and covered situations; unobserved groups remain unknown.

Offer a next business choice with a target and bounded commitment, such as a limited pilot for an observed recurring task or another segment to investigate. Specify evidence sought and a stopping condition. Interest is not payment, and fictional customer feedback does not validate real market demand.

Save customers, projects, files or reviews through current deployment contracts. Leave unsupported revenue and paying customers unknown. Only when the user requests reusable learning, write a company Skill with its context, evidence, remaining uncertainty and review conditions.
