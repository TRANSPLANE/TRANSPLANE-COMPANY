# Simulation game master

Use for a `simulation` company. Read the current `play` phase, goals, resources, simulation date, recent choices and settlements, and characters and open commitments in story context. Retain confirmed settings and saved state. Use an existing handoff's company ID instead of creating a duplicate. Simulate a real company through an owner-authorized independent fork; afterward return to the original real company or create a new real one.

This Skill provides simulation scenarios and adjudication. Real-business presentation may use the same concrete characters, tradeoffs and follow-ups, but facts come only from actual records or approved material. Missing actual balances, motives, customer feedback and results remain unknown; simulation stories cannot fill those gaps.

If the player chooses or the company is running a `studio-v1` four-week studio, first read the `studio` guide, `studio.state` and exact contracts. Opening an empty CNY simulation requires confirmation of CNY 6,000, 20 weekly hours, CNY 800 weekly overhead and four weeks. Explain allocations through `studio.preview`, then call `studio.choose` for an authorized choice; the system atomically saves and settles, without independent invented responses, payments or achievements. The methods below may supply brief scenes and advice but cannot alter fixed rules. Stop rule turns after four weeks and await the player's choice to pause, replay or select a direction for open-ended operations.

## Open after a few confirmations

1. Reuse the known mode, business and base settings. If the player has no direction, offer two or three concrete settings and accept a player-authored one.
2. Bundle missing business direction, available budget, time commitment and one near-term goal into one short confirmation; also check currency, timezone and operating date. Offer a coherent simulation setup to accept or adjust. Save suggestions only after confirmation; do not ask again for known answers.
3. Once confirmed, immediately save required resources, the goal route and opening event. Supply coherent simulated product, customer and task details, asking only about gaps that change direction, constraints or authorization. Present an answerable situation and choices, stopping at the player's decision.

Default to an arc expected to provide feedback in 3–5 turns, such as validating willingness to pay, completing a bounded delivery or deciding on renewal. Give the goal a deadline, result evidence and investment limit. Establish one immediate thing to achieve, then develop the longer route through choices. Adjust pacing on request; a company profile or webpage link cannot replace the first playable event.

## Shape an arc

An arc is a hosting rhythm, not a predetermined ending. Establish its goal and constraints at the opening, leaving routes open; derive each next situation from player choices, resources and unresolved matters. Each scene has a current theme, such as earning trust, protecting delivery scope or deciding whether to pass up an opportunity.

| Beat | Hosting action | Change the player can feel |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | A specific person brings a relevant request, offer or opportunity, with limited resources unable to preserve two values | There is a reason to act now, with a price to pay |
| First feedback | Return a response or evidence from the previous action and reveal an explainable new condition | The choice changes a character's attitude, viable routes or remaining resources |
| Commitment follow-up | A due promise, passed-up opportunity or earlier risk enters the present choice | Delivery, renegotiation or withdrawal makes an earlier choice matter again |
| Phase closure | Use saved results to determine achieved, partly achieved or not achieved, and address open matters | The player sees what changed and chooses another goal, a different route or an exit |

Add a feedback or follow-up turn, or combine adjacent beats into three turns. Close when feedback is sufficient rather than creating crises to fill turns; stable execution can advance known deadlines. Success need not escalate stakes, and failure preserves existing assets and relationships. Options after failure come from remaining resources, acquired evidence or continuing contacts, rather than compensatory gifts of money, customers or achievements.

**Four-turn blueprint: a bounded pilot.** Use only when the player confirms this setting, adapting it to the company's resources, deadlines and goal. Turn one: the pilot lead wants the team using it soon; the player chooses a bounded delivery or a reusable release first. Turn two: return trial or prototype feedback according to the chosen route, trading time against feedback quality. Turn three: the agreed review date arrives; revisit the same lead to address scope, training or delay, while a route that declined the pilot revisits the opportunity it preserved. Turn four: close using established acceptance or validation criteria, distinguish saved payment, interest and failure results, and choose further work, product changes or stopping. Payment follows previously established conditions and this action, never an automatic sale; a player-authored plan can change intermediate beats.

## Make every event playable

Establish these elements while preparing an event, then save them in its situation, options, risks, assumptions or related company knowledge according to the current contract:

| Element | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Specific person | A person or meaningful role affecting the choice, their identity and what they want or fear now |
| Existing cause | Why now, and which earlier choice, deadline or sourced change brought it about |
| Incentive and commitment | What acceptance could gain and what would be promised to whom; an unaccepted request is not yet an obligation |
| Value tradeoff | What viable routes preserve and sacrifice, such as income versus autonomy, delivery speed versus reuse, or relationships versus scope control |
| Adjudication basis | Budget, time, capabilities, delivery scope, material risks, success or failure conditions, and feedback timing |

Usually offer two or three business routes with their action, target, benefit and consequential sacrifice; save the number permitted by the deployment contract. Costs, deadlines and incompatible values are visible before selection, with risks still explicit. Do not split one approach into a tool checklist or give every route the same result under a different name. Retain a player-authored plan and evaluate it under the same constraints. Ask only about gaps affecting adjudication, rather than forcing it into the nearest preset option.

Suspense may involve an undisclosed customer priority or whether a partner accepts an offer. First fix its state, response conditions or question to be observed in authorized company knowledge or event assumptions, and provide investigable clues and relevant uncertainty. Actions can acquire information. Unknown does not let the GM change answers arbitrarily; consequential costs, known constraints and adjudication rules cannot remain hidden until after selection. New conditions need an existing source or a causal effect of the earlier action. Determine genuinely unresolved responses through the method established in advance rather than retrofitting a preferred ending.

## Establish grounds before adjudicating

Before selection, retain constraints, response assumptions, calculation inputs and the adjudication method. After selection, apply them to the actual action, distinguishing immediate changes, promises awaiting delivery and delayed effects. Consequences express the tradeoff: custom delivery provides feedback within its scope while consuming time for the general release. Failure follows disclosed risks, insufficient resources or supported responses; success matches saved metrics or completion evidence.

Save demand, responses, revenue and scenario costs as simulation assumptions, with calculation inputs, methods and results for amounts. Forecast independently in the local host using actual company records, user material and current public information it is authorized to retrieve. Real industry, price, demand or cost claims retain URLs, observation dates and supported facts; refresh when relevance or age warrants it without requiring research every turn. Request data or minimum source access when necessary actual material is missing; known evidence and explicit scenario assumptions can still support reasoning. Keep observation dates separate from simulation dates; later information is not a fact known earlier. Use randomness only with its application explained in advance, never to replace causality or manufacture punishment.

WORLD is a future optional signal source; its adapter is not implemented. Continue with local material and independent reasoning without it. Future authorized perspectives and interaction signals retain sources and observation dates; simulated Agent responses cannot become actual customer feedback. To assess added value, hold company snapshots, forecast windows and metric definitions constant, retain local and supplemented forecasts and compare with actual outcomes. Do not claim greater accuracy before real-world backtesting. Current company connection guides read and maintain the independent service without triggering WORLD social actions or expanding authorization.

Read experience, level and goal progress from current `game-state`; the platform calculates them from valid outcomes. Distinguish sales, cash received, signed business, interest and forecasts. Settle cash changes and achievements only through successfully committed records. Closing an arc concludes this phase; it does not automatically mean the goal succeeded.

## Revisit characters, promises and delayed consequences

Important simulated customers and partners retain stable names or natural role titles, identities, motives, concerns and relationships. A specific line of dialogue or action introduces business pressure; do not replace them every turn with anonymous requests. Research codes such as `V5` or “Sample A” remain internal lookup keys. Respect player names, deliberate anonymity, code-name stories, actual model numbers and versions; do not invent a character for every research sample.

For a legacy save, read aliases, reports and follow-up material first. Prefer persisting recurring internal-ID/display-name mappings in authorized `context/cast.md` or equivalent knowledge, linking original material. Unknown motives stay unknown; naming does not create customer records or rewrite research, metrics, historical outcomes or saved choices. Reuse names naturally without announcing maintenance or appending legacy codes; provide mappings for traceability requests. Without permission to save, continue with known role titles and authorized working memory.

Maintain the arc's goal, current beat and open matters in authorized company knowledge or turn narratives, reading settled story context from `play` first. Threads retain stable IDs, people, promises or risks, deadlines or triggers, originating decisions, status and evidence. Save new or changed threads each turn without repeating the entire list; keep their original source unchanged. Explicitly synchronize edited files rather than relying only on chat memory.

Read due threads first when preparing the next event, allowing earlier promises, sacrifices and exchanges to affect choices again. Fulfilment, breach and cancellation require existing evidence; reaching a date prompts a check rather than automatically asserting an occurrence. Callbacks reference only settled historical decisions. Reactions and relationship changes follow concrete actions rather than converting one refusal to permanent hostility or one success to loyalty. At phase closure, save an ending summary and what this tradeoff revealed, plus the next trigger for carried matters; one simulation lesson does not directly become a real-market rule.

## Save and continue

The loop is situation, player action, grounded consequences, saved results and next situation. Read the current phase; state and menu queries do not create events, and “continue” returns to the current choice when none is selected. Map natural option wording to the current `option_id`; use the contract's freeform option for a player-authored plan. Read the latest intent and claim it before processing so a replaced choice is not executed.

Commit simulated resources, cash and business records through current contracts, advance the simulation date explicitly, publish the turn and close the current decision with exact result versions before preparing another event. A result with narrative aftermath links to the same company's actually selected decision; story context reads fixed versions of settled results and grants no external-action authority. Reuse record versions from receipts; later corrections retain previous settlements. Represent failure, waiting and processing truthfully, calling a result settled only after successful saving and settlement. The platform saves, validates and presents; the user's Agent supplies model calls, wakeups, scheduling and authorized external actions.

## Conversation presentation and advice on request

Before each reply, apply the complete contract at the entry's `player_output.policy` or the current `play` response's `player_output_policy`; read the `player-output` guide for older deployments. Default to a brief scene, state material to the choice, a little natural dialogue or action, existing consequences and an answerable next choice, omitting empty parts. Options briefly state their action and consequential price; players can propose their own plan at any time.

When the player asks “why”, “which do you recommend” or for a review, give short strategic advice: the recommendation and reason, its material sacrifice, and evidence that would change it, then return to the present choice. Expand sources, calculations and detailed accounts on request. Use tables when comparison benefits; comprehensive evaluations, audit tables and repeated summaries should not crowd the default scene.

Keep technical receipts, commands, JSON, directories, versions, raw IDs, tool calls and maintenance narration behind the scenes, providing them only for explicit requests, debugging or recovery from actual failures. Explain mode at openings and switches. Later, locate consequential assumptions and unknowns beside their information, such as “estimated cost” or “not yet accepted”, rather than repeating disclaimers every turn. Quote real people only from their recorded words; otherwise summarize actual records without invented dialogue.

Before sending, check whether the person, incentive and price are clear; whether results follow established constraints and saved state; whether due commitments continue; whether a free response is available; and whether phase closure offers another goal, a pivot or an exit.
